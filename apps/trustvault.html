📓 About This Process
1
Upload the Death Certificate
The executor (designated trusted person) uploads an official government-issued death certificate. TrustVault records the file name and date. The certificate must be signed by a licensed medical professional or government authority.
2
Guardian Vote Begins
After the certificate is recorded, each guardian is notified via their request file. They independently confirm the death on their own copy of TrustVault. Once M-of-N guardians confirm, the vote is complete. This prevents a single person from fraudulently triggering distribution.
3
Challenge Period Elapses
If configured, a challenge period begins after all conditions are met. This gives any surviving party time to contest an erroneous trigger. After the challenge period, distribution files are generated automatically for each beneficiary.
⚠ For the executor: Do not proceed until you have the original or certified copy of the death certificate. Most jurisdictions issue 10–20 certified copies when registering a death — request enough copies as each institution (bank, exchange, probate court) will require one.

Requiring 2 of 3 guardian votes to confirm distribution.
How to choose M and N:
2-of-3 (recommended for most families): Three trusted people, any two can confirm. Protects against one guardian being unavailable, coerced, or passing away before you.
3-of-5: Five guardians, three must confirm. Higher security. Better for large estates or if your family is geographically spread across different countries.
1-of-1: Only acceptable if you have exactly one trusted person. High risk — if that person is unavailable, distribution is blocked indefinitely.
Who to choose: Guardians must be adults you deeply trust, ideally with different last names and living in different locations. Your your executor should be one. A close friend, your attorney, or a sibling are good choices for others. Never choose guardians who are also beneficiaries.

Seed Phrase & Private Key Security
The most sensitive piece — handled correctly it protects everything
Most Critical
▼
🚨 NEVER do these with your seed phrase: Never photograph it. Never type it into any computer or phone. Never email it. Never store it in cloud storage. Never enter it on any website — not even one that looks like Ledger or Coinbase. The seed phrase should ONLY ever be entered directly into a physical hardware device during recovery.
How to store your seed phrase safely:
Best: Stamp or engrave the 24 words onto a metal plate (Cryptosteel, Bilodal, or DIY stainless steel). Metal survives fire, floods, and decades of time. Store one copy in a fireproof safe at home and a second copy in a bank safety deposit box.
Acceptable: Write legibly on paper, seal in a waterproof pouch, store in fireproof safe. Keep a duplicate at a second location.
Never: Digital photos, screenshots, cloud notes (iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive), email drafts, password managers, USB drives — all are hackable. The seed phrase must ONLY ever exist on physical media.
Who should know where it is: Only [Your Executor Name] and your trusted crypto helper should know the location of the seed phrase storage. Not family members who may be pressured. Not attorneys who may not keep it secure.
